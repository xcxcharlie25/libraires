Pharmacy Network Optimization

Pharmacy network layer · synthetic data in the Optum claims schema

The problem in one paragraph

A client's members fill prescriptions at pharmacies today. Some of those pharmacies charge more than others for the same medicine, some infusions happen in hospitals when a clinic would do, and some stores in the network serve almost nobody. The model asks one question: what is the cheapest network that still serves every member, without asking members to accept more disruption than the business is willing to allow? It answers it many times, with different tolerance for disruption each time, and the answers form a curve the business can read.

How the model thinks

Money, in dollars

The drug bill at each member's assigned pharmacies, priced at that pharmacy's contract rate for that kind of drug, plus one dispensing fee per fill, plus the annual cost of keeping each pharmacy under contract. This is the only thing the model minimizes.

Disruption, in people and miles

Two things, weighted by how often a member visits. Moved or not: did their pharmacy change? Extra miles: how much farther is the new one than today's? Both carry a weight the business turns. Neither is a dollar value of a person's inconvenience; the weights are how we ask the model to become increasingly unwilling to inconvenience people.
minimize   drug bill + network cost  +  A × Σ visits × moved  +  λ × Σ visits × extra miles
Two dials, two curves. Turn A up and fewer people move; the curve shows what each level of stability costs in savings. Turn λ up and fewer people are sent farther; the curve shows what protecting travel costs. Every point on either curve is a complete, lawful network. The business picks the point.

What is guaranteed, never traded

Everyone served, in network

Every member, every drug they use, assigned to an open pharmacy of the right kind: retail for retail drugs, a specialty pharmacy for specialty drugs, an infusion site for infusions. Not a target. A rule the solver cannot break.

Nobody sent too far

No member is assigned a pharmacy more than 10 miles from home unless it is the one they already use. Home infusion and delivered specialty have no distance, because the care comes to the member.

Built for next year, not last year

Every footprint ZIP keeps a pharmacy within reach whether or not a member lives there today. No site is filled past 80% of capacity, leaving room for new members. Utilization trends by drug class put both today and tomorrow on the same footing.

Three layers of protection

Assignment for members who use a kind of pharmacy today: where does their known demand go. Member access for every current member: a retail store within reach is guaranteed even for members with no retail claims this year, allotted afterward, never counted as moved. Territory for members not yet enrolled: adequacy per ZIP across the client's observed footprint.

The standing rules

  • A member's retail drugs all go to one retail store; drugs split across sites only when their type forces it.
  • Mail members stay on mail; 30 and 90 day supply stay as today. Channel migration is a separate study.
  • Pharmacies closed to new patients keep only the members they serve today.
  • Chains contracted all or nothing stay together, within a state. Mail sites and any-willing-pharmacy states are always open.
  • Network quality, weighted by today's fills, cannot fall below a floor.
  • An infusion patient is never moved to a more expensive setting than today's: never from home or a clinic into a hospital.
  • Two candidate modes: CURRENT may only keep or drop today's pharmacies; ALL may also contract new ones. Both run; the difference is the value of contracting.

How to read the results

The ledger

Gross saving is today's drug bill minus the optimized drug bill, same members, same fills, same list prices; only the pharmacy changes. Net saving adds the change in network administration cost. Neither ever subtracts the disruption weights; those shape the answer, they are not expenses.

The bands

Every moved member is sorted by how much farther they now travel: as close or closer, up to 2 miles, 2 to 5, 5 to 8, 8 to 10. Each band shows count, the saving those moves produced, saving per move, and what a copay incentive for that band would cost. Delivered specialty and home infusion moves sit in their own rows: no travel. Retail and infusion are shown separately because an infusion move is worth a hundred retail moves.

Assumptions, stated plainly

  • Synthetic data. Every member, pharmacy, and claim is generated. The tables follow the Optum warehouse schema so real extracts drop in, but no number here is a real number.
  • Unmanaged baseline. The generator parks members at expensive stores and sends most infusions to hospitals, deliberately, so the levers have something to find. The shape of results is meaningful; the magnitude is not, until real claims set the baseline.
  • Site-of-care factors are assumptions. Hospital outpatient 1.6, physician office 1.15, infusion center 1.0, home 0.9. Under these modeled differentials, moving infusions out of hospitals produces some of the largest per-member savings. That is a modeled opportunity, not a realized one.
  • 100% steering. The model assumes every member goes where the network sends them. Real members choose. Results are addressable savings, to be discounted by an expected migration rate.
  • Fixed contract rates. No volume tiers: a chain's discount does not deepen with committed volume. The largest real retail lever is parked until contracting supplies rate cards.
  • One retail store per member. Members using two stores today are consolidated to the one they use most.
  • Straight-line miles, home to pharmacy. Not drive time.
  • Every site carries every drug of its type. No stocking, no limited distribution, no prior authorization, no continuity-of-care rules.
  • Specialty is delivered. Specialty pharmacies have no distance rule. Infusion capacity is in infusions per year by setting; home infusion is unlimited.
  • Footprint is the observed footprint. Territory adequacy protects new members within ZIPs where the client has members today, not new geographies.
  • Rebates are out. A manufacturer rebate is the same whichever pharmacy dispenses, so it does not change the network. Channel-dependent rebates would.
